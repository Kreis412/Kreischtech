# ContractorSight competitive research and product priorities

Research date: September 25, 2026. Internal working brief, not public competitive advertising. Recommendations are not implemented features or measured results.

## Decision

Compete on a connected, accessible workflow: capture jobsite evidence, review potential issues, account for the resulting scope and cost, assign work, and track the outcome. Price and implementation effort are part of that experience. An overlapping AI feature does not establish an equivalent overall product; conversely, the research does not establish that this combination is exclusive to ContractorSight.

Prioritize durable field capture, reviewed finding-to-estimate connections, understandable cost completeness, and permission-aware portfolio visibility. Add depth without forcing a field worker through every office feature.

## Research method and limits

Reviewed official product/help/pricing pages and accessible individual reviews on Capterra, Software Advice, and Apple's App Store across six competitors. Search results also surfaced G2 summaries and Reddit discussions; these were discovery aids, not evidence of complaint prevalence. No paid competitor accounts or hands-on comparison were used.

This is a qualitative sample, not a representative survey. Review sites can feature invited/incentivized reviews; some reviewers are outside construction. Prefer identifiable construction roles and specific descriptions. Do not treat syndicated reviews across sites as independent corroboration. Some App Store entries omit the year or show inconsistent dates; do not label those current confirmed defects. Historical complaints are useful regression scenarios, not proof a defect remains. Positive reviews and vendor responses were considered alongside criticism.

Feature documentation establishes advertised behavior, not independent proof of performance. Pricing is package-specific and can change. User-reported implementation fees in the thousands were not verified as a universal Procore charge. Keep subscription, implementation, training, migration, and add-on costs separate in comparisons.

## Evidence and implications

### 1. Procore: enterprise benchmark

App Store reviewers describe disruption from navigation redesigns and difficulty understanding pending uploads; a December 11, 2025 review also describes document revision and offline access frustrations. Other reviewers praise shared plans and the platform overall. Treat these as field reliability and navigation test cases, not a verdict that Procore is universally hard to use. [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/procore/id374930542?platform=iphone&see-all=reviews)

Procore advertises photo/video/notes analysis that drafts observations and recommended actions. This is real advertised overlap; package access and practical performance were not verified. [Field Observations Agent](https://www.procore.com/ai/agents/field-observations)

Its official pricing is an annual custom quote based on products and construction volume. It advertises unlimited users with a Field Productivity exception, and implementation services without a universal public setup fee. We should not describe its standard model as per-seat or invent a fixed onboarding price. [Pricing](https://www.procore.com/pricing)

Our response: stable navigation; persistent upload status; visible current revisions; source-linked decisions; an easy adoption path for a crew inside an existing larger organization. Enterprise integrations remain future development.

### 2. Buildertrend: integrated residential workflow benchmark

US App Store reviewers report interrupted upload problems and deeply nested navigation; one describes export difficulty and a price increase. These are individual experiences, with some dates lacking years. A Canadian March 5, 2024 review describes report/photo crashes, an explicitly historical example. [US reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/buildertrend/id504370616?platform=watch&see-all=reviews), [Canadian reviews](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/buildertrend/id504370616?platform=iphone&see-all=reviews)

Official information describes custom pricing, unlimited users, no free trial, and two onboarding tiers. The support page distinguishes initial setup from a weeks 3–12 guided onboarding phase. Beginning to use software and fully adopting financial workflows are different milestones. [FAQ](https://buildertrend.com/frequently-asked-questions/), [Support](https://buildertrend.com/support/)

Our response: a real sample project before payment, progressive setup, exportable records, recoverable drafts, and transparent renewal terms. Do not require complete financial configuration before someone can document a job.

### 3. JobTread: the closest estimating/adoption benchmark

Accessible Capterra reviews strongly praise support, estimating, customization, and job costing. Specific criticisms include selection workflows and messaging; several displayed October 2025 reviews are vendor-referred/incentivized. Evidence does not support characterizing JobTread as broadly unusable. [Reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/218503/JobTread/reviews/)

Its official offering includes personalized onboarding, training, all features, and free limited-access external/eligible field users. Free onboarding is therefore not exclusive to us. [Pricing](https://www.jobtread.com/pricing), [Onboarding details](https://www.jobtread.com/invite)

JobTread's September 10, 2026 announcement describes AI budget creation, auditing, and editing. It does not by itself demonstrate our intended photo-to-reviewed-scope workflow. [AI tools](https://www.jobtread.com/blog/jobtread-ai-tools-for-budgets-formulas-and-forms)

Our response: benchmark estimating and support against a strong competitor. Improve the evidence-to-budget handoff, not just the number of estimate fields. Compete on the full workflow and measured customer effort.

### 4. Contractor Foreman: breadth at a lower price

Capterra examples include Android camera limitations (October 2025), difficulty getting a portfolio overview (May 2026), and reporting/navigation complaints. Other customers praise value and customization. These are concrete test cases rather than established product-wide failures. [Reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/166113/Contractor-Foreman/reviews/)

Its official plans page starts at $49/month under annual billing and includes training offerings. Low price and broad features already have competition. [Plans](https://contractorforeman.com/plans/)

Our response: reliable camera entry on Android and iPhone, a focused manager exception list, and optional modules instead of a crowded default navigation. Test actual common workflows on multiple devices.

### 5. CompanyCam: photo workflow benchmark

Capterra reviewers praise easy photo sharing and client documentation. A July 9, 2026 reviewer reports confusion while merging accounts/photos; another wants property history to survive ownership changes. A November 17, 2025 review raises price and checklist-tier concerns. [Reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/171143/CompanyCam/)

CompanyCam explicitly documents offline photo/task/checklist work and a waiting-to-upload banner in its mobile app. Offline capture is an existing competitor capability. Its AI documentation covers reports and tasks generated from photos and supplied context; that alone does not prove an identical proposed-work assessment. [Offline help](https://help.companycam.com/en/articles/6828443-using-offline-mode), [AI guide](https://companycam.com/resources/user-guides/ai)

Our response: make photo capture equally dependable, then connect evidence to scope and cost. Preserve original records during merges; separate property/site identity from the customer occupying it. Add these only with explicit access boundaries.

### 6. Fieldwire: field execution benchmark

Capterra reviews praise mobile drawings, punch lists, and collaboration. A November 2020 billing complaint is historical, not evidence of today's billing behavior. An August 2026 Software Advice review praises usability while asking for more mobile functionality; it is incentivized and outside construction, so give it less weight. [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/142801/Fieldwire/reviews/), [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/construction/fieldwire-profile/)

Official pricing includes a free tier and paid per-user tiers; compare an entire team's required package rather than a starting price. [Pricing](https://www.fieldwire.com/pricing/)

Our response: clear field tasks tied to evidence/location, straightforward team pricing, and a phone workflow that handles daily work without sending users back to a desktop. Do not claim field simplicity is a new invention.

## Current ContractorSight baseline

The existing pilot contains projects/photos, AI draft findings with human decisions, linked work, materials/estimate worksheets, saved estimate copies, crew assignments, approved labor and expenses, equipment photo records, company reporting, and optional Joe. Dollar entry and help/onboarding text have recently improved.

These are foundations, not a completed chain. The manual Discovery Log and operations findings are separate concepts. The current browser explicitly tells users to reconnect before saving; a durable offline upload queue is not implemented. Costs reflect recorded entries, and an estimate snapshot is not a controlled customer-approved budget. Current roles are company-wide, not project-scoped field/customer permissions. A live pilot does not establish commercial-scale capacity or analysis accuracy.

Reviewed local app.js, operations.js, materials.js and pilot/architecture documentation. Older release documents contain pre-hosting statements and historical test counts; use them as historical notes rather than a current live-service audit. This research did not alter deployment or run new functionality tests.

## Strongest signature workflow

Example: a site photo plus intended work raises a drainage question. Joe identifies what is visible, what the user supplied, and what remains unknown. A person records the decision. The finding can then create an editable scope bundle covering relevant material, labor, equipment, disposal and verification allowances. The estimate shows the bundle as priced, excluded, covered elsewhere, or unresolved. Approved scope becomes assigned work; actual costs remain traceable to it.

This is the product hypothesis to validate. It is not a claim that the current pilot automates all these steps or that no competitor can provide them.

Avoid an unexplained 'AI confidence' percentage. Show evidence, missing inputs and specific next checks. A reported measurement is not an inferred measurement. A reviewed commercial decision does not turn an AI finding into an engineering certification.

## Processes that make the company better

1. Outcome-based onboarding: sample job, first photo, first reviewed finding and first estimate. Offer skip/import paths. Measure time to first useful result, not videos watched. No mandatory full cost-catalog setup before first use.
2. Optional Joe guidance: short, relevant suggestions at decision points; explain why a suggestion appears; dismiss/snooze/off. Never block ordinary work while AI runs. Never require paid inference for basic help.
3. Stable releases: preserve familiar locations for core actions; test navigation changes with field testers; offer a preview for major changes. Test account access and saved work before deployment; maintain a rollback/recovery procedure.
4. Pricing trust: publish inclusions, team limits, storage/AI allowances and paid extras. Show usage before limits. Obtain opt-in for paid overages. Offer reasonable notice for price changes and a clear cancellation/export path. Final economics remain to be validated.
5. Support that closes the loop: in-app issue report with consented diagnostic details, receipt, status and resolution notification. Prioritize lost work, access problems and incorrect money before cosmetic requests. Do not promise 24/7 human support without staffing.
6. Learn from positive reviews: responsive help and predictable financial workflows matter as much as new features. A suggestion board should group duplicates and show investigating/planned/shipped without treating every request as a promise.
7. Maintain an evidence register: attach dates, versions, segment and source to competitive claims. Recheck before publishing comparisons. Sales demonstrations must show currently working capabilities and state package limits.

## Proposed acceptance tests and success measures

These are future targets, not results achieved during this research.

| Test | Proposed pass condition |
|---|---|
| First-use test | At least 4 of 5 initially untrained testers create a project, save a photo, log a finding and enter a cost without coaching. Small-sample signal, not market proof. |
| Fast manual capture | From an open project, photo plus short note saved locally within 30 seconds at the 90th percentile in the pilot; record camera/device/network conditions separately. |
| Interrupted work | Phone call, app backgrounding, connection loss and reopen preserve saved drafts in supported conditions; one server record per item after retry. Explicitly test browser storage refusal/eviction. |
| Cross-device | Uploaded record appears on desktop within 5 seconds after server acknowledgement under the defined test network; status remains honest while pending. |
| Financial completeness | Unknown price never silently totals as zero; exclusions require reasons; known costs are distinguished from committed costs and forecast. |
| Estimate traceability | Every reviewed finding can be traced to its scope disposition; repeated actions cannot add the same scope twice without an explicit new item. |
| Analysis quality | Use held-out residential and commercial cases labeled by qualified reviewers. Score supported observations, unsupported assertions, missed material concerns and next-question usefulness. Set launch thresholds before running the evaluation. |
| Role isolation | A field user cannot obtain another project's records or internal rates through UI, direct APIs, exports or cached drafts. Membership removal is enforced at the server. |
| Manager overview | Tester can identify which project needs attention and why in under one minute without opening every project. |
| Browser/device coverage | Real Android Chrome and iPhone Safari plus desktop. Test currency, camera, keyboard overlap, upload recovery, large text and poor connectivity. |
| Recovery | Restore accounts, projects, photos and usage history from an off-host backup; verify access isolation after restore. |

Track support requests per active company, failed/successful upload ratios, unresolved sync age, onboarding completion and time to reviewed estimate. For 'missed scope caught,' count only human-confirmed omissions with documented value; do not report all AI suggestions or total project value as money saved. Measure support effort and AI/storage cost per company before finalizing tiers.

## Company overview: core requirement added during research

The owner explicitly requested company reporting comparable to competitors, including YTD revenue. The product must support business oversight as well as field capture. Keep a concise Company overview and place detailed charts and configurable analysis on Statistics, preserving the earlier product direction.

Official benchmarks: JobTread offers financial dashboards, custom reporting and spreadsheet exports; Procore supports reporting across projects; Buildertrend describes budgets with actual, committed and projected costs and profitability. These are different levels of financial completeness, not interchangeable cash totals. [JobTread reporting](https://www.jobtread.com/features/reporting-and-dashboards), [Procore Company 360](https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/company-level/reports), [Buildertrend budgets](https://buildertrend.com/help-article/job-costing-budget-overview/)

Current code check: company.mjs and public/company.js already calculate year/quarter cash receipts, disbursements, net movement, monthly charts, customer concentration, job duration, NPS and estimate gap statistics. These are manually recorded cash/operational measures. There is no complete invoice/receivables, revenue recognition or controlled contract-backlog model. Do not relabel net cash movement as profit or cash receipts as earned revenue.

| Required overview | Display and behavior | Data dependency |
|---|---|---|
| YTD financial summary | Revenue on a stated basis, separately displayed cash collected, direct job costs and overhead. Show exact period and data freshness. | Invoice/credit records and an explicit revenue basis; advanced earned revenue requires accounting/WIP inputs. |
| Period comparisons | Month, quarter, YTD, fiscal YTD and custom dates; compare with matching prior-year elapsed dates, not an entire prior year. | Company fiscal calendar/timezone and tested period boundaries. |
| Profitability | Gross profit/margin and operating result only when underlying revenue and cost coverage support them; separately show forecast job margin. | Consistent costs, revenue basis and overhead; account for missing entries instead of inventing zeroes. |
| Receivables | Unpaid and overdue invoices, aging buckets and partial payments; drill into invoice and customer. | Issued invoices, due dates, allocations, credits, write-offs and voids. |
| Contracted work remaining | Approved contract value plus approved changes minus completed/earned work on the chosen basis; pending estimates and unapproved changes remain separate. | Approved contracts/changes and reliable completion or earned-value records. |
| Project financial health | Original/current budget, actual cost, outstanding commitments, forecast remaining cost and expected final margin across jobs. | Prevent double counting purchase commitments when bills become actual costs; manual forecast allowed with author/date. |
| Commercial oversight | Filter by project manager, division, branch, customer, job type and status; authorized users drill from company total to project evidence. | Extend organization fields and enforce financial/project permissions server-side. |
| Operational action list | Overdue work, missing manager updates, unpriced discoveries, pending approvals and equipment downtime. | Record owner, next action, due date and source; no unsupported predictive claims. |
| Charts and exports | Monthly revenue/cost bars, comparable YTD trend, cost breakdown, budget vs actual; export exactly the selected scope/period. | Exclude voids/refunds correctly; show unavailable rather than misleading pie shares. |

Suggested first dashboard delivery: cash collected YTD and quarterly comparisons using existing records; project cost totals and unresolved estimate gaps; explicit 'Not tracked yet' for invoice-derived metrics until their records exist. Follow with invoices/payment allocations, controlled budgets and contract changes so financial parity is substantive. Never fill missing revenue or receivable cards with cash-based substitutes merely to look complete.

Additional tests: deposits and partial payments cannot duplicate billed revenue; credit/refund and void corrections reconcile; approved labor cannot be counted again as payroll expense; unapproved changes do not increase booked work; cancelled contracts leave backlog correctly; prior-period reports handle revisions consistently; restricted roles cannot access totals or source transactions. Document metric formulas and source records in an accessible 'How this is calculated' explanation.

Company overview is a P0 product track alongside reliable capture and finding-to-estimate work. Mature financial parity is staged because the necessary records must exist before trustworthy metrics can be displayed.

## Project return on cost requested by owner

Include a simple Project profitability panel. Prefer 'Return on job cost' with a short explanation instead of an ambiguous ROI label. This is a job-level return ratio, not annualized investment return, cash-on-cash return, or company net profit.

- Estimated project profit = estimated selling price minus estimated job costs.
- Estimated return on job cost = estimated project profit / estimated job costs x 100.
- Estimated margin = estimated project profit / estimated selling price x 100.
- Forecast final profit = approved contract value (including approved changes) minus actual costs and forecast remaining costs. Avoid counting costs already represented in actuals again in remaining commitments.
- Completed-job return = final job revenue on the stated basis minus complete actual job costs, divided by those costs. Label provisional when records are incomplete. Do not use cash collected as a silent substitute for revenue.
- Example: $13,000 selling price and $10,000 costs produce $3,000 estimated profit, 30% return on cost and approximately 23.1% margin. A 29.9% markup produces 29.9% estimated return on cost and approximately 23.0% margin only for the cost base to which that markup applies.

Show whether overhead is included, with costs broken into materials, labor burden, equipment, subcontractors and other job costs. Preserve the user's custom markup; do not market the 29.9% default as an independently verified regional average. Zero or missing cost/revenue denominators display 'Not available', not infinity or a misleading zero. Negative returns remain visible. Forecast and estimated cards must not be presented as realized profit.

Implementation order: estimated return using the existing reviewed worksheet first (small calculation/UI change); forecast and completed-job returns depend on approved revenue and complete cost records. Tests should cover zero costs, losses, refunds, excluded/unpriced items, rounding, duplicated labor, pending changes and final-versus-estimated status. This section specifies future work; the research task did not add live dashboard calculations.

## Recommended next milestone

Finish the photo-to-reviewed-estimate chain alongside reliable draft/photo recovery. Include narrow field permissions and account recovery as paid-release gates. Validate the flow using one residential remodeling case and one commercial trade case, with both a field worker and a manager. Keep existing project management, equipment and reporting functionality, but surface each capability where it supports that workflow.

Do not spend the next cycle adding a large collection of disconnected features. Successful differentiation should be demonstrated by fewer steps, retained evidence, explicit cost decisions, and a reasonable total cost to adopt.
